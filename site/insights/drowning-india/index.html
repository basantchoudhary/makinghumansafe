Where and when people drown in India

From 75 water incidents we checked one by one (2022 to 2026), 210 deaths. Each record links to a news report, and we confirmed the quoted sentence is really in it. This is not every drowning in India (official figures run to tens of thousands a year); it is a careful sample that shows patterns.

When: May and June are the worst months

Summer holidays bring crowds to beaches and waterfalls, and pre-monsoon and monsoon swells make the sea rough. Waterfalls and rivers are most dangerous in the monsoon (June to September), when water can rise within minutes.

A calm sea is not a safe sea

We checked the waves on the day of 41 beach drownings: those days were no rougher than the three months around them (average 51st percentile; only 15% among the roughest quarter of days, against 25% by chance). People drown on ordinary-looking days, pulled by rip currents or a sudden wave. A calm forecast is not a reason to swim.

Who, and why

  • Most victims were groups of young friends, students or colleagues on a trip. Often several people were swept away together, and some drowned trying to rescue a friend.
  • In 10 incidents the report says people went in despite a red flag, a barrier, a ban or a lifeguard's warning.
  • A lifeguard is mentioned as present in 11 incidents: lifeguards save lives, but only where people swim near them.

What to do

Swim only near lifeguards and between their flags; never after drinking or in the evening; stay off wet rocks; if caught in a rip current, swim sideways, not back; if a friend is pulled out, shout for help and throw something that floats instead of swimming after them. See the pictures.
