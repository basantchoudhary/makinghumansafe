Stay safe, in pictures

Each picture teaches one thing. Share the one that matters for your next trip or your home.

The cast: Amma who waits at home, Bro who takes risks, Meera who travels solo, Dadaji the grandfather, Chhotu the child, and Palm the open-hand guide.

Rip current escape: a calm-looking gap in the waves pulls a swimmer out to sea. Don't swim straight back; swim sideways, parallel to the beach, then back in. A red flag means no swimming.

Monsoon sea: from June to September dark clouds bring big swells and rip currents. A red flag is up. Amma and Chhotu watch from the sand instead of swimming.

Waterfall danger: rain far upstream can make the water rise within minutes even when it is sunny here. Bro takes a selfie on a wet rock as the water rises. Get off rocks when the water turns brown or gets louder.

Boat safety: a family in a boat, each wearing a buckled orange life jacket. Count the life jackets before you board; one per person, worn, not kept under the seat.

Gas leak at home: if you smell gas, don't touch any switches, turn off the cylinder regulator, open doors and windows, go outside, and call 1906, India's LPG emergency number.

Elderly falls at home happen in three places: a wet bathroom floor, loose rugs, and stairs without a rail. The fix: grab bars, anti-slip mats, night lights and a stair rail. Dadaji stands nearby with his walking stick.

Taxi scam: a driver shouts a price three times too high. Meera checks the fair fare on her phone and the official fare board before getting in, and uses the prepaid counter or the official app.

Report helper: Meera reports what happened, where and when on her phone with a map pin. Palm reminds her: no names and no faces. Your report helps the next person.

Be the co-pilot: Amma drives with her eyes on the road while Bro, in the passenger seat, taps the phone to report a hazard. The driver never touches the phone.

In danger? Call your emergency number. 112 India and Europe · 911 US and Canada · 000 Australia · 999 UK · 1906 LPG gas leak (India)
