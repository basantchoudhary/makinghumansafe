Bengaluru: road safety, black spots and help

How many people die on the roads here, where the black spots are, and what to do, with a source for every fact.

Bengaluru road safety, simply

Bengaluru's roads: highest since 2007. Who dies: 59% motorcyclists. Worst: evenings, late nights and weekends. Avoid night driving.

1How bad is it?

933 killed in 2026: the highest since 2007 (981 that year). About 3 people die on the roads every day.

From: Bengaluru Traffic Police, accident cases registered in Bengaluru city 1976 to 2026

2Who is dying?

59% of road fatalities were among motorcyclists.

From: Bengaluru Traffic Police with BIGRS, Road Safety Report 2023; ThePrint: civic negligence killed 20 in Bengaluru in 2023, highest for the 4th year (NCRB)

3When?

43% of all crash fatalities occurred between 6pm and 10pm, and 12am to 2am.

From: Bengaluru Traffic Police with BIGRS, Road Safety Report 2023

4Where?

BTP’s North Division recorded the highest number of fatal crashes with pedestrians.

From: Bengaluru Traffic Police with BIGRS, Road Safety Report 2023; Making Human Safe checked incident list; WRI India via The News Minute: are school kids safe on Bengaluru roads? (17 Nov 2021)

5Why?

Nearly 60-65% of road traffic fatalities are closely associated with human behavioural risk factors.

From: Bengaluru Traffic Police with BIGRS, Road Safety Report 2023; Citizen Matters: key risk factors behind road crashes in Bengaluru (7 Nov 2024); Deccan Herald: Bengaluru records second-highest pedestrian deaths among million-plus cities in 2024

6So what do I do?

Avoid driving at night and on weekends to reduce risk.

From: Bengaluru Traffic Police with BIGRS, Road Safety Report 2023

The three lessons that repeat most in our checked crashes:

  1. Slow down, most of all at night and on empty roads. (speeding or loss of control: 85 of 120 crashes)
  2. Stay out of heavy vehicles' blind spots; never overtake them on the left near junctions. (lorry, tipper, bus or truck: 85 of 120 crashes)
  3. Take extra care after dark: dip high beams, watch for people walking and parked vehicles. (night or early hours: 74 of 120 crashes)

What to do

  • Two-wheelers: a helmet for rider and pillion, buckled. In rain, slow down: potholes fill with water and look like flat road.
  • Phone: never while driving. A co-pilot or a dashboard mount with voice directions only.
  • Night: dip high beams, watch for unlit parked vehicles, and don't drive tired.
  • Junctions and exits: expect late lane changes and two-wheelers filtering between lanes.
  • Crash ahead? Park well off the road with hazard lights, call 112, and don't move an injured person unless there is fire or traffic danger.
