Bengaluru: road safety, black spots and help

How many people die on the roads here, where the black spots are, and what to do, with a source for every fact.

Coverage: built from open sources (travel guides, news, official alerts, open maps). Many incidents go unreported, so an absence of reports is not proof of safety.

The story in one picture

Between January and September 2026, 836 people died in road accidents. 43% of these fatalities occurred between 6pm and 10pm, and 12am to 2am. About 47% happened on Saturday, Sunday, and Monday, with Sundays seeing the peak. Two-wheeler users remain the most vulnerable group. Head injuries account for 60-70% of fatal cases. Only 2% of children under two use restraints. Bengaluru has 64 black spots, with 11 on the Outer Ring Road. These areas lack safety features. 30.6% of vehicles exceeded safe speeds. Overspeeding and dangerous driving are major contributors. Police have intensified enforcement. 47% of riders who remembered the campaign reported following speed limits.

What to do

  • Two-wheelers: a helmet for rider and pillion, buckled. In rain, slow down: potholes fill with water and look like flat road.
  • Phone: never while driving. A co-pilot or a dashboard mount with voice directions only.
  • Night: dip high beams, watch for unlit parked vehicles, and don't drive tired.
  • Junctions and exits: expect late lane changes and two-wheelers filtering between lanes.
  • Crash ahead? Park well off the road with hazard lights, call 112, and don't move an injured person unless there is fire or traffic danger.
