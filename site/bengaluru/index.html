Bengaluru: road safety, black spots and help

How many people die on the roads here, where the black spots are, and what to do, with a source for every fact.

Coverage: built from open sources (travel guides, news, official alerts, open maps). Many incidents go unreported, so an absence of reports is not proof of safety.

Bengaluru roads: 933 people killed from January to October 2026, about 3 a day. That is already more than any full year since 2021 (highest: 910 in 2023). The traffic police list 64 accident black spots in the city. Our checked news records here: 3 road incidents, mostly two-wheeler riders. Wear a helmet (rider and pillion), never use the phone while driving, and slow down in rain: potholes hide under water.

What to do

  • Two-wheelers: a helmet for rider and pillion, buckled. In rain, slow down: potholes fill with water and look like flat road.
  • Phone: never while driving. A co-pilot or a dashboard mount with voice directions only.
  • Night: dip high beams, watch for unlit parked vehicles, and don't drive tired.
  • Junctions and exits: expect late lane changes and two-wheelers filtering between lanes.
  • Crash ahead? Park well off the road with hazard lights, call 112, and don't move an injured person unless there is fire or traffic danger.

If you are travelling with…

Women

  • Share your live location with someone you trust when travelling at night.
  • Use the prepaid taxi counter or a booked cab; note the vehicle number before getting in.
  • Stay on lit, busy roads after dark; avoid empty beach stretches at night.
  • Call 112 in an emergency or 181 for the women's helpline.

Teens

  • Don't swim after drinking, at river mouths or outside the safe season.
  • Go in groups and tell someone where you're going and when you'll be back.
  • Wear a helmet on rented scooters.

Kids

  • Swim only at beaches with lifeguards, and only in flagged areas.
  • Keep children within arm's reach in the water.
  • Carry water and avoid the midday sun.

Elderly

  • Know the nearest hospital to where you stay (see Help nearby).
  • Carry a list of medicines and an emergency contact.
  • Be wary of calls claiming to be police or officials asking for money: hang up and call 112.
