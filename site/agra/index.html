Agra: safety, scams and fair fares

What to watch out for, what taxis should cost, and where help is, with a source for every fact.

Coverage: built from open sources (travel guides, news, official alerts, open maps). Many incidents go unreported, so an absence of reports is not proof of safety.

Before you go

Taxis

We could not find an officially notified per-km fare for Agra's autos, e-rickshaws or taxis, so fares are negotiated; the one official figure is the Taj Mahal golf cart (₹25 for Indian citizens, free with a foreigner's entry ticket). Because there is no meter fare, the risk is an inflated quote or a currency mix-up. Agree the fare in rupees before the ride, and note that only non-polluting vehicles may go within 500 m of the Taj.

Scooters, bikes and roads

2 reported incidents since 2026, 4 deaths. Latest: 2026-11-14.

Where: Agra-Lucknow expressway, Ramleela Maidan gate, Rakabganj

  • Wear a helmet, every ride; it's the law and it saves lives.
  • Carry your driving licence; rent only from shops that ask for it.
  • Photograph the scooter before you take it, to avoid false damage claims.
  • Don't ride after drinking or on unlit roads at night.

Theft, assault and harassment

1 reported incident since 2025. Latest: 2025-04-03.

Where: Shamshan Ghat Road, near Taj Mahal

  • Keep valuables out of sight in crowds, markets and on beaches.
  • Share your live location with someone you trust at night.

From the Wikivoyage guide to Agra, written by travellers. Quoted under CC BY-SA 4.0; source: https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Agra, fetched 2026-11-13.

By taxi

  • You can either book a taxi from the hotel or book one directly outside the railway station. There is usually a government-authorised taxi stand; however, it may be hard to find, and the locals present at the station (looking for gullible tourists) will not help you find it. ₹1500/day for 8 hours. It may be more costly to book through a hotel as hotels do have their in fares. It is better to negotiate with the driver directly or book through an online car rental portal.

By rickshaw

  • Auto-rickshaws and cycle rickshaws are available everywhere. Remember to agree on fares clearly in advance. Foreigners should bargain everywhere and bargain hard. Generally, things are available at 40% of the initially quoted fares. Normally, charges for e-rickshaws are ₹150-200 per e-rickshaw, which can accommodate up to 4 people. As a guide, an auto rickshaw from Agra Cantonment station to the Taj is about ₹200 (at least in off-season), and a cycle rickshaw from the Taj to Agra Fort is ₹40.

Stay safe

  • Don't leave cash or any valuables in the hotel room. Cross check all hotel, restaurant and lounge bills for errors.
  • Never pay anyone for anything upfront, including taxi drivers.
  • If you decide to purchase anything, beware that most items are cheap replicas of original items and not likely to last long. The toys are really poorly put together so do not buy them!
  • During the winter season, the weather of Agra is unpredictable and temperature may go as low as freezing; be well prepared.
  • Some unscrupulous dealers of carpets use the classic 'bait and switch'. If you buy something, insist on carrying it yourself else what arrives in the post might not be what you bargained for. A carpet shop named 'Kanu carpets' is particularly infamous for this. It is prudent to steer clear of shady looking establishments.

  • No polluting vehicles are allowed within 500 m of the Taj Mahal. Parking is at Shilpgram for the east gate and Amrood ka Teela for the west gate, with battery buses and golf carts from parking to the gate. UP Police helpline is 112. (Taj Mahal official site (tajmahal.gov.in))

If you are travelling with…

Women

  • Share your live location with someone you trust when travelling at night.
  • Use the prepaid taxi counter or a booked cab; note the vehicle number before getting in.
  • Stay on lit, busy roads after dark; avoid empty beach stretches at night.
  • Call 112 in an emergency or 181 for the women's helpline.

Teens

  • Don't swim after drinking, at river mouths or outside the safe season.
  • Go in groups and tell someone where you're going and when you'll be back.
  • Wear a helmet on rented scooters.

Kids

  • Swim only at beaches with lifeguards, and only in flagged areas.
  • Keep children within arm's reach in the water.
  • Carry water and avoid the midday sun.

Elderly

  • Know the nearest hospital to where you stay (see Help nearby).
  • Carry a list of medicines and an emergency contact.
  • Be wary of calls claiming to be police or officials asking for money: hang up and call 112.
