Varanasi: safety, scams and fair fares

What to watch out for, what taxis should cost, and where help is, with a source for every fact.

Coverage: built from open sources (travel guides, news, official alerts, open maps). Many incidents go unreported, so an absence of reports is not proof of safety.

Before you go

Taxis

We found no officially notified per-km fare for Varanasi's autos, e-rickshaws or taxis; e-rickshaws mostly run shared on traffic-police-assigned routes at a per-seat fare. On busy days drivers multiply this: Amar Ujala reported ₹30-40 per seat becoming up to ₹100 on the Godowlia to Kal Bhairav route over New Year 2025. Ask locals for the usual per-seat fare, agree whether a quote is per person or for the whole vehicle, and note the driver's name and phone number, which must be displayed in the vehicle.

Activities: what has happened, and what to do

Swimming (sea, rivers and ghats)

2 reported incidents since 2025, 2 deaths. Latest: 2026-09-30.

Where: Dandi Ghat, Hanuman Ghat, Bhelupur

  • Swim only where lifeguards are on duty and inside the flagged area; a red flag means no swimming.
  • Rivers can look calm and still pull hard below the surface: at ghats, stay on the steps or hold the chains.
  • Don't swim after drinking, at night, or alone.
  • If caught in a current, don't fight it: swim parallel to the shore, then back in.
  • Keep children within arm's reach in the water.

Water sports (rafting, parasailing, boats, coracles)

1 reported incident since 2025. Latest: 2025-01-31.

Where: Man Mandir Ghat

  • Use only licensed operators with a visible registration; ask for it.
  • Always wear a life jacket and a helmet for rafting, and check the harness and straps yourself before parasailing.
  • Don't go out in rough sea or strong wind, even if the operator says it's fine.
  • Agree the price and duration before you start.

Scooters, bikes and roads

No checked incidents in our records yet.

  • Wear a helmet, every ride; it's the law and it saves lives.
  • Carry your driving licence; rent only from shops that ask for it.
  • Photograph the scooter before you take it, to avoid false damage claims.
  • Don't ride after drinking or on unlit roads at night.

Read the travel guide's full safety section

From the Wikivoyage guide to Varanasi, written by travellers. Quoted under CC BY-SA 4.0; source: https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Varanasi, fetched 2026-11-13.

By rickshaw

  • The chaotic streets of Varanasi are sometimes too much to handle on foot. Therefore, it will often be convenient to travel by cycle rickshaw, E-rickshaw or auto-rickshaw. A short journey of a few kilometres should cost under ₹50, while a longer journey within the city, such as between the ghats and the train station, will cost ₹100. Further journeys, such as to Sarnath (10 km), cost ₹500 there and back (with a one- to two-hour waiting period) or ₹300 one way by auto-rickshaw.
  • There is a pre-paid auto-rickshaw stand at Varanasi Junction NR .

By car or taxi

  • While taxis and cars-for-hire do exist, you can hire them for airport/railway/bus station transfers and for major city tour specially for BHU,Sarnath and Ramnager tour. You can travel from other cities to Varanasi by car as it's well connected to the major cities of the country.
  • Big Cab Varanasi. Big Cab Varanasi is also present in Varanasi. (updated Apr 2024)

Stay safe

  • Violent crime is rare, but still do be careful in the lanes after dark.(Safe maximum times)
  • Carry a light or phone; power outages are now rare, but due to sleeping cows and broken paving stones, the alleys are hard enough to navigate in daylight, let alone in pitch dark.
  • Though generally well behaved, packs of stray dogs can become aggressive at night
  • Rickshaw and taxi scams are common in Varanasi, and the driver will inevitably tell you that the hotel that you wish to go to has burned down, is flooded, or closed. Don't believe him. Drivers receive commission from hotels for bringing in new guests, and this is one way to trick newcomers to going to these places. Don't get annoyed, but see the exchange as playful banter and part of the Varanasi experience. However, if the driver continuously refuses to follow your instructions, threaten to get out of the rickshaw. If after all this you still end up to a different place, just refuse to pay until you arrive at your hotel. The same procedure will need to be followed when sight seeing, as drivers will inevitably try to take you to handicraft stores, from which they receive commission. If calling for a pickup from a more respectable hostel, be wary that other taxi drivers may listen in to your phone conversation then tell another taxi driver who will pick you up pretending to be your hostel, then take you to a commission charging hostel.
  • As you approach Manikarnika Ghat, you will be approached by touts posing as cremators or volunteers in a local hospice. These people will explain the cremation process and even lead you to a good viewpoint. They will then take advantage of your emotions by asking you to pay for some wood to burn the body of someone who can't afford it. They will usually insist on receiving ₹500; if you offer less, it will initially be declined. This is a scam. Either tell him you have no money or that you don't feel comfortable donating.
  • Varanasi is one of the most polluted cities in the world. Many websites show air quality readings taken in the early morning, when pollution is at its lowest. Hourly air quality data is available here. Keep an eye on air quality data and consider wearing a (certified) mask, especially if you are spending an extended period of time in the city.

If you are travelling with…

Women

  • Share your live location with someone you trust when travelling at night.
  • Use the prepaid taxi counter or a booked cab; note the vehicle number before getting in.
  • Stay on lit, busy roads after dark; avoid empty beach stretches at night.
  • Call 112 in an emergency or 181 for the women's helpline.

Teens

  • Don't swim after drinking, at river mouths or outside the safe season.
  • Go in groups and tell someone where you're going and when you'll be back.
  • Wear a helmet on rented scooters.

Kids

  • Swim only at beaches with lifeguards, and only in flagged areas.
  • Keep children within arm's reach in the water.
  • Carry water and avoid the midday sun.

Elderly

  • Know the nearest hospital to where you stay (see Help nearby).
  • Carry a list of medicines and an emergency contact.
  • Be wary of calls claiming to be police or officials asking for money: hang up and call 112.
