Before you go

Taxis

We found no official fixed fare for Hampi autos or coracles; every figure below is a traveller-reported price, and they vary widely (a full-day auto was quoted ₹1,500 to ₹2,000 in Nov 2023, and coracle rides from ₹150 to ₹750 per person depending on the spot and length). The common pattern is negotiated prices with no rate card, so first-time visitors pay the top of the range. Take the local bus from Hospet (₹17 in April 2023) and fix the price and ride length before boarding an auto or coracle.

Activities: what has happened, and what to do

Incidents are counted only when the source article was opened and found to name the place and the event (3 of 3 stored incidents passed this check). Many incidents are never reported.

Swimming (sea, rivers and ghats)

1 reported incident since 2025, 1 death. Latest: 2025-02-19.

Where: Tungabhadra River

  • Swim only where lifeguards are on duty and inside the flagged area; a red flag means no swimming.
  • Rivers can look calm and still pull hard below the surface: at ghats, stay on the steps or hold the chains.
  • Don't swim after drinking, at night, or alone.
  • If caught in a current, don't fight it: swim parallel to the shore, then back in.
  • Keep children within arm's reach in the water.

Water sports (rafting, parasailing, boats, coracles)

No checked incidents in our records yet.

  • Use only licensed operators with a visible registration; ask for it.
  • Always wear a life jacket and a helmet for rafting, and check the harness and straps yourself before parasailing.
  • Don't go out in rough sea or strong wind, even if the operator says it's fine.
  • Agree the price and duration before you start.

Waterfalls and treks

1 reported incident since 2025. Latest: 2025-12-24.

Where: Mahanavami mound

  • Don't swim or climb on rocks near waterfalls, especially after rain: water rises fast and rocks are slippery.
  • Check whether the area is open; many falls close in the monsoon.
  • Go with a guide or a group, carry water, and tell someone your plan.
  • Know how far the nearest hospital is (see Help nearby).

Scooters, bikes and roads

No checked incidents in our records yet.

  • Wear a helmet, every ride; it's the law and it saves lives.
  • Carry your driving licence; rent only from shops that ask for it.
  • Photograph the scooter before you take it, to avoid false damage claims.
  • Don't ride after drinking or on unlit roads at night.

From the Wikivoyage guide to Hampi, written by travellers. Quoted under CC BY-SA 4.0; source: https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hampi, fetched 2026-11-13.

By auto rickshaw

  • A convenient way to see all the sights without climbing the hills is by rickshaw, as the very aggressive drivers will very insistingly suggest as soon as you show your face anywhere.
  • Bargain with your rickshaw driver for a fixed rate (₹400-500 for a full day seems to be okay) and then hire an official Karnataka Travel guide (at the tourist office, they have fixed rates: 4 hr costs ₹500, 8h is ₹800) It costs a little bit, but the explanations help you to see more than just stones and temples. To get an estimate of cost and content, you can talk to the guides before coming down to Hampi. The guides willingly share their cellular numbers and email addresses.
  • As of Nov 2023, the full day charge for an auto-rickshaw varied from ₹1500 to ₹2000 depending on your negotiation skills.

By boat

  • Many guest houses are on the other side of the river in Sanapur, Hanumanahalli or in between the two, in Virupapur Gaddi. To access them, you need to cross river with the ferry. The people operating it have a monopoly, so the service is extortionate and the operators rather rude. ₹50 per crossing, you'll have to wait until there are enough people to fill the boat. Last boat around 5PM, so forget about coming back after watching the sunset on the hill. If you cannot get on the boat, an auto will take you other side via Hospet-Hugli-Hanumanhalli for at least ₹1000, it's a 40-km tour. During moderate/high rains, gates of Tungbhadra dam are opened which increases the water level and water flow in river. In that case, one might get stranded on the other side of river (Virupapur Gadde area).

Stay safe

  • Visitors should be sure to watch out for monkeys. They are notorious for snatching and running away with visitors' handbags, cameras, and other loose belongings.
  • Keeping mosquito repellent on hand is a good idea.
  • Be extra careful when you choose to explore rocky places, especially when you are trying to climb over boulders.
  • Avoid travelling to Hampi during the months of April and May, as temperature can get as high as 43°C (110°F). If you are planning to visit it during summer, remember to carry sufficient water so as to avoid the risk of dehydration. Bottled drinking water is easily available. You may not be able to get chilled bottles (as electricity will sometimes cut out).

Respect

  • Out of deference to the sacred nature of the site, do not consume drugs and alcohol in the area.
  • When visiting the temple or ruins of former temples, cover up and do not show too much skin. The locals will not stop you, but may bear a silent grudge. Be a good guest.
  • Please visit any monument only for the sake of visiting, but not for any other purpose. Please do not scribble or climb or sit on any monuments.They have stood rain and sun for centuries and have to continue to stand for many more centuries.

If you are travelling with…

Women

  • Share your live location with someone you trust when travelling at night.
  • Use the prepaid taxi counter or a booked cab; note the vehicle number before getting in.
  • Stay on lit, busy roads after dark; avoid empty beach stretches at night.
  • Call 112 in an emergency or 181 for the women's helpline.

Teens

  • Don't swim after drinking, at river mouths or outside the safe season.
  • Go in groups and tell someone where you're going and when you'll be back.
  • Wear a helmet on rented scooters.

Kids

  • Swim only at beaches with lifeguards, and only in flagged areas.
  • Keep children within arm's reach in the water.
  • Carry water and avoid the midday sun.

Elderly

  • Know the nearest hospital to where you stay (see Help nearby).
  • Carry a list of medicines and an emergency contact.
  • Be wary of calls claiming to be police or officials asking for money: hang up and call 112.
