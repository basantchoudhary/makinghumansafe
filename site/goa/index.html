Who's going?

  • Children only in knee-deep water, with an adult within arm's reach the whole time.
  • A life jacket for anyone who can't swim well, even in shallow water.
  • Stay away from rocks, river mouths and the calm-looking gaps between waves (rip currents).
  • Nearest hospital: Mathew Braganza Hospital, 0.3 km from Baga Beach.
  • Most drownings in our records are groups of young friends. One person stays out of the water and watches.
  • No swimming after drinking, and never at dusk or night.
  • If a friend is pulled out, don't swim after them: shout for help, call 112, throw anything that floats.
  • Swim only where other people are around, in daylight.
  • Tell someone where you are going and when you'll be back.
  • Isolated coves and rocks are where help is furthest away.
  • Waves can knock you over even in knee-deep water: stay at the edge.
  • Wet rocks and steps are slippery: wear grippy footwear and use a hand to steady yourself.
  • Carry your medicines and an emergency contact card.
  • Nearest hospital: Mathew Braganza Hospital, 0.3 km from Baga Beach.

Before you go

Taxis

Goa's official taxi fares are contested: different government documents and statements give different per-km rates, and app-based fares (GoaMiles) are disputed by taxi unions. That confusion is why overcharging is common. Buy a prepaid slip at the airport counter, never from touts, and agree the fare before you get in.

Activities: what has happened, and what to do

Incidents are counted only when the source article was opened and found to name the place and the event (3 of 3 stored incidents passed this check). Many incidents are never reported.

Swimming (sea, rivers and ghats)

1 reported incident since 2026. Latest: 2026-11-11.

Where: Arambol beach

  • Swim only where lifeguards are on duty and inside the flagged area; a red flag means no swimming.
  • Rivers can look calm and still pull hard below the surface: at ghats, stay on the steps or hold the chains.
  • Don't swim after drinking, at night, or alone.
  • If caught in a current, don't fight it: swim parallel to the shore, then back in.
  • Keep children within arm's reach in the water.

Water sports (rafting, parasailing, boats, coracles)

No checked incidents in our records yet.

  • Use only licensed operators with a visible registration; ask for it.
  • Always wear a life jacket and a helmet for rafting, and check the harness and straps yourself before parasailing.
  • Don't go out in rough sea or strong wind, even if the operator says it's fine.
  • Agree the price and duration before you start.

Waterfalls and treks

No checked incidents in our records yet.

  • Don't swim or climb on rocks near waterfalls, especially after rain: water rises fast and rocks are slippery.
  • Check whether the area is open; many falls close in the monsoon.
  • Go with a guide or a group, carry water, and tell someone your plan.
  • Know how far the nearest hospital is (see Help nearby).

Scooters, bikes and roads

1 reported incident since 2026, 1 death. Latest: 2026-11-08.

Where: St Cruz

  • Wear a helmet, every ride; it's the law and it saves lives.
  • Carry your driving licence; rent only from shops that ask for it.
  • Photograph the scooter before you take it, to avoid false damage claims.
  • Don't ride after drinking or on unlit roads at night.

By taxi

  • In Goa you will easily get taxi services outside of a hotel or can book online taxi booking. There is no taxi service from companies like Ola or Uber, but the ride sharing app Goa Miles is available for booking local taxis on iOS and Android.

Stay safe

  • There have been some serious incidents involving foreign nationals in the early 2020s, including sexual assaults.
  • Be careful when wading at the beach as undertow riptide currents can be strong in certain beaches. Avoid the mouths of all rivers (such as the Mandovi River at Miramar), especially at low tide when the flow of the water current out to sea is the strongest. And just don't get into the water at all in the off season. The safe swimming period in Goa is November to early May.
  • Avoid contact with unprocessed cashew nuts as they contain an irritant ('urusiol') also present in poison ivy. The cashew apple is edible when ripe.
  • Goans are very friendly and helpful. If you meet a driver you like, get their contact information and book them for subsequent trips. Travel guides can be expensive and have been known to dupe foreign visitors. Beware of guides offering to take you to a disco with lots of attractive girls, who will dance with you. This is a scam. Don't go to cheap disco clubs, which are mostly in Calangute, Baga and Anjuna. They steal your money and put you in trouble. The rates are mostly around 500 INR; avoid this.
  • Any scam that offers a free ride in return for a "prize" will be guaranteed to disappoint.
  • It is illegal to drink alcohol in public places in Goa. If you drink alcohol outside the limits of a licensed premises, you could be fined or given a prison sentence. You can drink within the limits of a registered beach shack or bar, for example, but not on an open beach or road.
  • Beware the 'ear doctors', who are more likely to accost men than women and 'produce' some tiny revolting creature, supposedly from your ear, for which they then offer a 'cure'. It is, however, humorous to read the cards they print up promoting themselves.
  • While travelling by train, beware of pickpockets, strangers who offer you snacks or tea, and other such people who make trains in India a regular hunting ground. Do not take off your shoes in non-A/C coaches, or they might get stolen. The same goes with all your valuables.
  • Don't trust travel agents who say that a train is fully booked. They want you to hire a car that costs more and provides them a kickback. A better thing to do is to check out the details yourself on the Indian Railways website. Also, you can book your railway ticket online[dead link].
  • Temperatures in winter and summer can be extreme, so do not forget sunscreen.
  • Goa now has a number — ☏ 108 for medical emergencies. This service is run by the GVK EMRI (Emergency Management and Research Institute) and is based out of Goa Medical College (Bambolim) and has ambulances posted at various parts of Goa. These ambulances are fully equipped and have trained paramedics.

If you are travelling with…

Women

  • Share your live location with someone you trust when travelling at night.
  • Use the prepaid taxi counter or a booked cab; note the vehicle number before getting in.
  • Stay on lit, busy roads after dark; avoid empty beach stretches at night.
  • Call 112 in an emergency or 181 for the women's helpline.

Teens

  • Don't swim after drinking, at river mouths or outside the safe season.
  • Go in groups and tell someone where you're going and when you'll be back.
  • Wear a helmet on rented scooters.

Kids

  • Swim only at beaches with lifeguards, and only in flagged areas.
  • Keep children within arm's reach in the water.
  • Carry water and avoid the midday sun.

Elderly

  • Know the nearest hospital to where you stay (see Help nearby).
  • Carry a list of medicines and an emergency contact.
  • Be wary of calls claiming to be police or officials asking for money: hang up and call 112.
